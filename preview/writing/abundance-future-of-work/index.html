Abundance won’t distribute itself

Whatever happens to Musk’s 2036 prediction, how AI-driven abundance reaches people and work depends on decisions leaders make.

October 18, 2026 · 2 min read

Musk says money won’t matter by 2036. Whatever you think of the date, the decisions about work are still ours.

On a slow Peloton ride I listened to Elon Musk’s long interview with The Economist. One line stayed with me: “Money won’t matter in 2036.”

I’m not going to defend the date. The question underneath is the interesting one: if AI makes expertise cheap and robotics scales what we can produce, what happens to work?

My view: scarcity shifts. It doesn’t vanish. Cheaper intelligence doesn’t make energy, materials, good locations or human attention unlimited. Producing more doesn’t settle who owns the assets or who gets access to the output. Smith and Ricardo’s questions about production and distribution still apply, even if some of their assumptions don’t.

For most people, a job is how economic output becomes income. If some work needs less human labor, how do those people share in the value? Lower prices, new work, wider ownership or income transfers could all help. None of them follows automatically from a better model. A promise of future abundance doesn’t pay the bills during a career transition.

Work is also more than income. It gives structure, relationships, recognition and a place to contribute. More freedom could be very positive. Losing a valued role without a credible next step is a different experience.

Inside a company, the same question shows up at a smaller scale. When a team can deliver the same result with less effort, someone has to decide how to use that capacity: better service, growth, learning or a lighter workload. That’s a leadership decision. The technology won’t make it for us.

What I’d put on the leadership agenda now, whatever you think of 2036:

  • Start with tasks and bottlenecks before deciding which jobs change.
  • Agree upfront how productivity gains will be used, including the investment to move people into new work.
  • Protect entry-level routes and supervised learning.
  • Say what’s changing, what’s still uncertain and when decisions will be reviewed.

What stayed with me after the ride wasn’t a world without money. It was the idea that human labor may stop being the main constraint on what we can achieve. Whether that’s good news for most people depends on the organizations and institutions we build around it.

Sources: The Economist · The full-length interview with Elon Musk; The Economist · Musk’s prediction about money in 2036. The workplace implications are my interpretation of a possible scenario, not a forecast.
