Onboarding is done when the new colleague can contribute

Design onboarding backward from a first real contribution, with the manager owning expectations.

October 13, 2026 · 1 min read

Equipment and access matter. So do priorities, relationships and permission to ask.

The onboarding checklist can be complete while a new starter still doesn’t know what to do first or who to ask. To them it’s one arrival, even if several functions deliver it.

HR can connect the preparation without owning every task. The manager still sets expectations, and colleagues still need to make room for questions. AI can support a guide or routine information. It can’t build the working relationship on the manager’s behalf.

Where I’d start: define a sensible first contribution for the role and work backward through the access, knowledge and relationships it requires. After week one, ask the new colleague which obstacles the plan missed.

The test: don’t turn speed into pressure to skip learning. Contribution should grow with clarity, support and the safeguards the role needs.
