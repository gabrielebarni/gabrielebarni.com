Build a workforce plan you can adjust

Plan the workforce across adoption scenarios with clear triggers, instead of defending a single long-term number.

October 13, 2026 · 1 min read

Different adoption speeds need different responses, not false precision.

A long-term workforce number looks reassuring on a slide. Its value depends on the assumptions underneath: demand, technology, adoption, process change and the capacity to train or redeploy people.

Those factors won’t move together. Separate them and find the decisions that hold up across several scenarios. The point is knowing what to do now and what evidence would change the plan, so nobody ends up defending a forecast after its assumptions have moved.

Where I’d start: for one function, compare three scenarios: limited assistance, widespread assistance and controlled automation. Describe the skills, delivery constraints and review workload in each. Agree clear triggers for hiring, development or redeployment.

The test: external forecasts, like the WEF survey of more than 1,000 employers, give context. They don’t replace local work analysis or proper conversations with affected employees.
