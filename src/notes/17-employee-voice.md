A process diagram rarely captures all the workarounds that keep a service running. The people doing the job can usually explain where the formal process stops and the practical judgement begins.

## Be clear about the room for influence

That knowledge is valuable before procurement and design decisions are fixed. Asking for feedback afterwards can leave people feeling they are being invited to endorse a choice. I would make clear what they can influence, what is constrained and how the input will reach the decision-makers.

## Close the feedback loop

Bring frontline users, managers and relevant employee representatives into the design discussion. Show realistic failures as well as the intended process. Afterwards, explain what changed, what did not and why.

Enthusiastic volunteers are not the whole workforce. Include different shifts, levels of digital confidence and ways of accessing the service.

<summary>Research behind this note</summary>

OECD surveys in finance and manufacturing across seven countries found training and worker consultation associated with better reported AI outcomes. The association does not prove that consultation caused the improvement.
