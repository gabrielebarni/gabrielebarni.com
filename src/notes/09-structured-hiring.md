A hiring team wants stronger candidates, faster decisions and fewer rounds. Yet the interviewers may still be assessing different versions of the job. Adding AI at that point can produce more material without improving alignment.

## Start with the business requirement

The first conversation should be about the work: what must this person deliver, which capabilities are critical and what evidence would demonstrate them? An AI-generated interview guide can support that discussion. It should not replace it, or provide a sophisticated justification for a preference formed afterwards.

## Calibrate the interviewers

Agree three critical competencies and comparable questions. Ask interviewers to score fictional answers independently against clear behavioural criteria, then discuss the differences. Have a subject-matter expert review any AI suggestions.

Style, accent and confidence can influence impressions without demonstrating capability. Keep the assessment job-related, accessible and explicit.

<details>
<summary>Research behind this note</summary>

US Office of Personnel Management guidance links structured, job-related interviews with stronger rater agreement and validity than loosely structured interviews. Structure includes consistent questions and scoring criteria.

Source: [US OPM · Structured interviews](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/other-assessment-methods/structured-interviews/) (practice guidance)
