Before putting an AI adviser in front of every employee, I would look at the colleagues already handling the service. Where do they lose time? Finding the right policy, interpreting an exception or preparing a clear response?

## A manageable first use case

An assistant may help with the search and drafting, while the agent keeps responsibility for the answer. That gives the organisation a bounded opportunity to learn. Research from customer support is encouraging in some respects, but it is not an HR business case. We still need evidence from our own work.

## Make the comparison credible

Choose a low-risk queue and establish the baseline. During the pilot, review a sample of answers for accuracy, applicability and clarity without identifying which used AI. Include the time spent checking and correcting the output.

Look beyond the average. Experienced agents, new starters and difficult case categories may experience quite different results.

<details>
<summary>Research behind this note</summary>

Research on 5,172 customer-support agents found uneven gains from AI assistance: less experienced workers benefited more, while experienced workers saw smaller speed gains and some quality declines. This was not an HR trial.

Source: [Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond · Generative AI at Work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771v2) (2024 revision)

</details>
