When a leadership team asks what AI means for headcount, it is tempting to reach for a forecast. I would first ask the team to describe the work that needs to be done and where the current constraints sit.

## Translate capability into a work decision

A role combines tasks, relationships, judgement and accountability. Automating a first draft may leave more time for analysis, or create additional review. The business still needs to decide what the role should deliver, which responsibilities remain and what people need to learn. Those choices cannot be read directly from an occupational exposure index.

## Build the view with the team

Map one team’s recurring activities. Identify where assistance could help, where controlled automation might be appropriate and where it adds little. Include dependencies, review effort and implementation timing before translating the exercise into workforce assumptions.

Two people with the same title may do substantially different work. Avoid applying a generic score to individual capability or performance.

<summary>Research behind this note</summary>

The ILO's 2025 occupational-exposure index combines task-level analysis, expert input and model predictions. It measures potential exposure; it does not establish that an exposed occupation will disappear.
