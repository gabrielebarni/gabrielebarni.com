A polished answer can be reassuring, especially when somebody needs a quick decision. In HR, that is precisely why we need to see what supports it. Confidence in the language is not enough.

## What a trustworthy response needs

I would expect a policy assistant to identify the approved document, its effective date and the population it covers. A link helps, but the answer must actually reflect the source. If essential context is missing, asking a further question is better service than filling the gap with an assumption.

## Test uncertainty as well as correctness

Test ordinary questions alongside outdated guidance, contradictory documents and cases with no approved answer. Check whether the assistant knows when to stop. Repeat those tests when either the policy or the model changes.

Confirm the minimum information needed. Do not guess an employee’s location, contract status or eligibility from an ambiguous request.
