The first pilot sets expectations for what follows. I would choose something important enough to matter, but narrow enough to understand when it works and when it fails.

## Choose a problem we can evaluate

A named business owner, a clear service problem and access to the people doing the work are more valuable than an ambitious launch statement. The objective is to learn how to improve delivery responsibly, then decide whether the approach deserves further investment.

## Use the first three months to earn the next decision

In the first month, map the service, involve users, establish the baseline and repair the knowledge. In the second, test safely and pilot with clear support and escalation. In the third, review quality, total effort and employee feedback, then make the next investment decision.

The timetable is a planning aid, not a commitment to launch regardless. Unresolved data, controls or service risks are reasons to pause.
