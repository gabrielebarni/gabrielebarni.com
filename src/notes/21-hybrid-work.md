An attendance target is easy to communicate. It is less useful when a team has not agreed which activities benefit from being together. Equally, flexibility alone will not resolve a real coordination problem.

## Make the purpose of presence explicit

I would ask the team where physical presence improves the work: difficult decisions, practical learning, relationship building or activities that depend on shared equipment. Then identify what needs uninterrupted focus. Research can inform the discussion, but the operating context still matters.

## Review the arrangement against the work

Agree the purpose of shared time, response expectations and how decisions are documented. Review delivery, collaboration and access to development over a meaningful period. Check whether colleagues outside the room receive the information they need.

Neither presence nor remote status is a reliable assessment of contribution on its own. Address the actual delivery problem rather than using location as its proxy.

<summary>Research behind this note</summary>

A randomised trial of 1,612 Trip.com employees found that two home-working days per week reduced attrition and improved satisfaction without damaging measured performance. The study concerned graduate employees in one company, not every role or remote model.

Source: [Bloom, Han & Liang · Hybrid work trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07500-2) (Nature, 2024)
