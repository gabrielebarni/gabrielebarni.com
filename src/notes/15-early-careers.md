A junior colleague can now produce a convincing first draft very quickly. The difficult question comes afterwards: can they explain it, recognise an exception and know when the result should not be used?

## Design the learning as well as the output

Some repetitive work deserves to disappear. Some also exposes people to the details through which judgement develops. I would separate those two functions before changing an entry-level role. The aim is to give newcomers useful practice and proper feedback, rather than preserve manual effort for its own sake.

## Check what the person can explain

Identify a few assignments that develop judgement. Ask the learner to attempt a case, compare approaches and discuss the differences with an experienced colleague. Include safe opportunities to work with less assistance and handle a variation.

Watch whether understanding is growing alongside output. A polished answer can hide a gap that only becomes visible when the situation changes.
